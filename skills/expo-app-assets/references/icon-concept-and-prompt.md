# App icon concept and prompt

Use this when the app has no approved source icon.

## Core rules

Design a real consumer app icon, not a corporate logo pasted onto a rounded tile.

**One memorable idea over feature explanation. Silhouette over detail. Brand character over category cliché. Concept before rendering style.**

Inspect the product purpose, audience, personality, interface, colors, and imagery. The strongest concept may be literal, suggestive, or completely abstract. Do not penalize an abstract icon because it does not explain the product immediately; memorability may matter more than category description.

- one dominant subject filling roughly 60-85% of the icon;
- strong silhouette, very few internal details, and one memorable quirk;
- two to four main colors with clear figure/background contrast;
- centered, asymmetric, cropped, oversized, or edge-emerging composition when useful;
- no default 3D, gradient, mascot, monogram, or literal category symbol.

## Exploration board

When the user wants options, show the full prompt and get approval to generate one board with six to nine numbered, crop-friendly square cells. Define the required diversity of concept families and silhouettes, but do not prescribe every individual metaphor unless the user requests specific directions.

Deliberately vary both concept family and rendering treatment: flat graphic, unusual mascot or creature, bold outline, geometric form, negative space, cropped character, minimal monochrome, controlled gradient, or soft dimensional. These are directions, not mandatory templates.

The board should look like different consumer apps created different identities for the same brief. At least half the options must differ in their underlying visual metaphor, not merely their rendering family. Do not render one symbol nine ways or apply the same clay object, pale background, extrusion, and drop shadow to every cell.

Use the **black-silhouette test**: if two foregrounds look nearly identical when filled black, replace one. Related or literal ideas are allowed when they remain useful choices. Let the user select the winner; recommendations are optional.

Use equal square cells with generous separation. Allow option numbers only. No descriptions, phone mockups, or decorative presentation inside the board.

## Avoid logo in a box

Reject generic corporate marks, generic monograms, stock interlocking or infinity forms, thin symbolic line art, badges, and emblems that feel pasted onto an app tile. Distinctive letterforms and interlocking forms remain valid concepts. A background is part of the icon composition; it must not merely frame a floating logo.

Ask: **Would someone remember this after seeing it for two seconds?** If not, replace it.

Mascots may be one unusual shape, two eyes, and a tiny expression. Do not automatically add faces. Dimensional work is optional; when used, keep one material, simple lighting, and restrained depth. Avoid generic AI clay objects, excessive glow, glass, chrome, bloom, and shadows.

## Final prompt and source

Show the complete copy-ready prompt before generation. Specify `1024x1024`, output type, product brief, personality, palette, composition, diversity, small-size readability, and exclusions. Do not bake platform corner masks into final artwork.

State whether the output is an **exploration board**, **transparent source subject**, or **flattened opaque master**. After selection, regenerate the chosen concept as a standalone 1024x1024 source; never use a board crop as the production master. Prefer one approved transparent source for consistent iOS, Android, monochrome, splash, and favicon variants.

Before accepting, verify one dominant idea, distinct silhouette, personality, 32px readability, app-icon character rather than corporate-logo character, and a rendering style that supports instead of replaces the concept.
