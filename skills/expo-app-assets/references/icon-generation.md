# Icon Generation

Use this reference when generating app-icon artwork or when translating the same generation knowledge into a prompt for an external image model.

## Generation goal

Create a memorable consumer app identity, not merely a category symbol or logo. The icon should feel designed specifically for its square canvas and remain recognizable at small size.

Think in this order:

**Product → Emotion → Identity → Behavior → Composition → Rendering**

Do not start with rendering style.

## Invent the identity

Understand what the app does and the feeling it should communicate, then invent the visual solution. Do not automatically use the most obvious category symbol.

Familiar objects are allowed, but transform them into something ownable through unusual scale, aggressive crop, edge emergence, overlap, wrapping, splitting, merging, transformation, negative space, unexpected character, or interaction with the frame.

Prefer one memorable visual idea over several pieces of symbolism. The concept should be understandable visually rather than requiring an explanation.

## Generate real variety

For exploration boards, invent the concepts yourself. Do not create several stylistic variations of one idea.

Explore genuinely different identity territories where useful: recognizable or transformed objects, characters or creatures, cropped subjects, bold silhouettes, negative-space ideas, tactile objects, graphic scenes, and expressive abstractions. These are possibilities, not required slots.

Vary the visual behavior too: **peek, emerge, crop, split, merge, wrap, overlap, fold, float, transform, stretch, frame, or cut through negative space.**

Two concepts with different colors but essentially the same silhouette and composition are not different concepts. At least half of an exploration should normally contain a subject a person could name aloud. Allow occasional wildcard concepts that prioritize memorability over literal category recognition.

## Compose for an app icon

Build around one dominant visual mass. Prefer bold silhouettes, large simple forms, meaningful asymmetry, intentional cropping, strong foreground/background relationships, minimal interior detail, roughly two to four important colors, and clear recognition at 32px.

Use the square as part of the design. The subject can enter from an edge, disappear behind it, fill the canvas, create negative space, wrap another form, or transform across the composition.

Do not default to **small centered symbol + colored or gradient background**. The background should participate in the identity rather than merely hold it.

## Render at icon scale

Simplify aggressively. A successful icon should resolve into a few strong shapes when viewed quickly.

Use the treatment that best serves the concept: clean flat graphic, controlled gradient, polished dimensional form, selective translucency or glass, tactile material, bold graphic illustration, minimal silhouette, or expressive abstraction.

Do not force every concept into one house style. Across a board, vary depth, material, palette, lighting, and graphical treatment when doing so strengthens the ideas.

Keep surfaces, geometry, edges, and lighting deliberate and polished. Compress recognizable subjects into app-icon language rather than detailed scenes or editorial illustrations.

## Preserve character

Simple does not mean generic. Keep one distinctive feature: unusual proportion, unexpected crop, strange silhouette, expressive gesture, clever transformation, distinctive negative space, surprising material, or an unusual interaction between forms. Remove details that do not support it.

## Avoid generation traps

Reject corporate logos in rounded squares, generic monograms, stock category symbols, meaningless interlocking marks, tiny centered marks, repeated constructions, generic clay objects, excessive glass or chrome, excessive glow, detailed cartoon scenes, sticker artwork, busy detail, and coordinated icon packs sharing one material, background, lighting, or palette.

Mascots and characters are welcome when they have a reason to exist. Do not automatically put eyes on objects.

## Exploration board

Present multiple concepts on one clean board using equal square cells, generous white gutters, small option numbers only, and crop-friendly compositions. Do not add captions, explanations, or device mockups.

The concepts should look as though different talented consumer-app teams independently answered the same brief.

Before accepting the board, mentally convert each concept to a black silhouette. If several become nearly identical, increase the concept or composition diversity. Also reject a board that accidentally repeats one rendering style.

## Final source

An exploration board is for choosing, not production. After selection, regenerate the chosen identity alone while preserving its subject, proportions, composition, palette, and character.

Produce a clean 1024×1024 square source with no option number, board gutters, device mockup, fake store badge, baked platform corner mask, or text unless integral and explicitly approved. Increase fidelity and polish without redesigning the selected concept.

## When direct image generation is unavailable

Use this guidance to create a concise copy-ready image-generation prompt. Describe the product, personality, desired diversity, composition principles, and quality bar without prescribing every concept. Give the external image model room to invent.

Tell the user that invoking `expo-app-assets` directly in an image-capable model generally preserves more context and produces better results than relaying a generated prompt. Never claim artwork exists until an actual generated image has been provided.
