# Icon Concept

Use this reference when an app does not yet have an approved icon concept.

The goal is to discover a memorable visual identity before deciding how it should be rendered.

## Understand the app

Identify:

- what the app helps people do;
- who it is for;
- the emotion or personality it should communicate;
- any existing brand language worth preserving.

Reduce this to a short creative idea. Do not immediately translate the app category into its most obvious symbol.

## Find the identity

Ask:

**What could this app become visually?**

Explore different identity archetypes where appropriate:

- recognizable object;
- transformed familiar object;
- character or creature;
- cropped subject;
- bold silhouette;
- negative-space idea;
- graphic scene;
- tactile object;
- expressive abstraction;
- unexpected metaphor.

These are creative territories, not required slots.

The subject itself is not enough. Give the idea a visual behavior:

**peek, emerge, crop, split, merge, wrap, overlap, fold, float, stretch, transform, frame, or cut through negative space.**

Think:

**Subject + Behavior = Concept**

A lipstick is only a lipstick. A lipstick cutting through oversized lips, emerging from an edge, melting into another form, or becoming part of a character can become an identity.

## Create meaningful variety

When multiple concepts are requested, vary the underlying idea rather than making style variations.

Change:

- subject;
- archetype;
- silhouette;
- visual behavior;
- composition;
- literal vs abstract thinking.

Do not solve six concepts with the same construction.

At least half of an exploration should normally have a subject someone could name aloud. Allow one or two wildcard ideas that prioritize personality and memorability over literal category recognition.

## Use familiar symbols carefully

Obvious category imagery is not forbidden. Reject the obvious **treatment**, not necessarily the obvious object.

A familiar symbol can work when transformed through unusual scale, cropping, personification, negative space, interaction, or combination.

Avoid concepts that are merely:

- category symbol centered in a square;
- generic monogram;
- corporate mark;
- stock icon;
- meaningless interlocking geometry;
- symbolism that requires explanation.

## Test each idea

A strong concept should answer yes to most of these:

**Memorable** — Would someone remember it after two seconds?

**Distinct** — Does it have a recognizable silhouette or behavior?

**Ownable** — Does it feel like this app rather than any app in the category?

**Simple** — Can the core idea survive at small size?

**Expressive** — Does it communicate some personality or emotion?

**Different** — Is it genuinely different from the other concepts?

If two ideas would become almost identical as black silhouettes, they are too similar.

## Exploration handoff

Do not over-design the concepts in text.

Once there are enough distinct territories, pass the product, personality, and creative directions to [icon-generation.md](icon-generation.md).

Let image generation resolve the exact shapes, materials, colors, lighting, and visual details.

The concept stage decides **what makes the icon memorable**.

The generation stage decides **how that idea becomes beautiful artwork**.
