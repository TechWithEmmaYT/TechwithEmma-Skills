# App Icon Styles

Use this reference to create visual diversity, not rigid templates.

## Core principle

**Identity before illustration. Concept before style.**

Do not ask only “what object represents this app?”

Ask:
**“What visual identity could this app own?”**

## Concept archetypes

### Character Mark
A simple expressive creature/personality with a recognizable silhouette.

### Abstract Organism
An invented blob, ghost, cell, droplet, cloud, or living shape with personality.

### Expressive Object
A familiar object altered through expression, proportion, motion, or unusual behavior.

### Transformation
One form visually becomes another. Best when the transition communicates the product indirectly.

### Negative Space
The memorable idea exists partly in what is missing.

### Cropped Identity
An oversized subject enters from an edge or is intentionally cropped.

### Geometric Identity
Simple geometry arranged into an ownable shape rather than a generic corporate mark.

### Symbolic Scene
A tiny visual world: path, horizon, opening, portal, landscape, orbit, etc.

### Visual Metaphor
Represent the product benefit or feeling rather than the literal category.

### Letterform Character
A letter becomes a distinctive shape or personality. Avoid generic monograms.

### Tactile Object
One simple dimensional object with convincing material and restrained lighting.

### Bold Silhouette
Extremely reduced form recognizable almost entirely from its outline.

## Visual behaviors

Concepts can:

- emerge from an edge
- hide/reveal
- wrap
- overlap
- transform
- stretch
- orbit
- fold
- split
- merge
- peek
- float
- cut through negative space
- interact with the icon boundary

Behavior should make the concept more memorable, not merely decorative.

## Rendering families

Choose what supports the idea:

Flat graphic · bold outline · minimal monochrome · controlled gradient · soft dimensional · tactile · geometric · illustrated · selective glow.

Never default the entire board to one treatment.

## Diversity rule

Two concepts for one app must differ in:

1. underlying metaphor/archetype, AND
2. silhouette/composition.

Different colors are not different concepts.

Different 3D renders of the same symbol are not different concepts.

## Cliché filter

Obvious category symbols are starting points, not finished concepts.

If an icon could be generated simply from:

“[category] + icon”

it probably needs another idea.

Literal concepts are acceptable when they contain a memorable transformation, character, crop, interaction, or visual quirk.

## Consumer-app test

Good icons should feel:

simple,
confident,
playful when appropriate,
visually immediate,
ownable,
recognizable at small size.

Avoid making every icon “premium” through shadows and gradients.

Premium comes from **clarity + taste + identity**.
