# Motion Concept

Use this reference before designing or implementing motion.

The goal is not to make the interface move more.

The goal is to make important moments feel more understandable, responsive,
memorable and characteristic of the product.

## Motion identity

Infer how the product should feel while moving.

Possible qualities include:

- precise;
- calm;
- energetic;
- tactile;
- playful;
- luxurious;
- technical;
- fluid;
- organic;
- magical;
- restrained;
- physical;
- expressive.

Do not reuse one motion personality across unrelated products.

A banking app may need controlled settling and precise numerical transitions.

A children's app may support more elasticity and celebration.

A sleep app may use slow atmospheric movement and almost no bounce.

A beauty app may emphasize transformation, reveal and fluid continuity.

## Attention map

Inspect the user flow and identify moments such as:

- first impression;
- primary action;
- selection;
- navigation;
- transformation;
- loading;
- progress;
- success;
- failure;
- data change;
- gesture;
- empty state;
- media interaction.

Not every moment needs animation.

Prioritize interactions where motion can explain or emotionally strengthen what
is happening.

## Signature interactions

Look for 1–3 interactions that could become part of the app's identity.

Ask:

**What interaction should the user remember?**

Examples of conceptual opportunities:

- scanning;
- completing;
- swiping;
- transforming;
- matching;
- depositing;
- recording;
- revealing;
- creating;
- progressing.

Do not begin with animation names.

Begin with the product action.

## Motion concept formula

Think:

**UI Moment + User Intent + Motion Behavior = Signature Interaction**

Example:

User submits an AI photo transformation.

Not:

"Scale the button."

Instead:

The control compresses under touch, processing emerges from the image,
transformation travels through the content, the new state resolves, and the
comparison control becomes available.

That is a motion concept.

## Choreography

One event may coordinate several visual reactions.

Examples:

Press progress may drive:

- control scale;
- icon displacement;
- surface depth;
- surrounding emphasis;
- haptic threshold.

Scroll progress may drive:

- hero size;
- title position;
- background;
- blur;
- navigation chrome;
- sticky content.

Gesture progress may drive:

- translation;
- rotation;
- neighboring-card response;
- indicator opacity;
- destination preview.

Prefer coordinated responses over unrelated effects.

## Restraint

Premium motion is selective.

Use motion where it:

- clarifies;
- guides;
- confirms;
- maintains continuity;
- communicates physicality;
- rewards;
- creates product personality.

Avoid movement that merely decorates.

A static element is acceptable when motion adds no value.
